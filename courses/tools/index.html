01 / communication contract

Will these endpoints match?

The publisher offers a policy; the subscriber requests a minimum. Test reliability and durability separately, then together.

Inspect each policy

PolicyOffered → requestedResult

The rule

Match = reliability compatible AND durability compatible Best effort → reliable: no Reliable → best effort: yes Volatile → transient local: no Transient local → volatile: yes

What this result means

A match here means only these two policies are compatible. It does not prove that endpoints exist, types match, discovery succeeds, data arrives on time, or the payload is correct.

Transient-local history is owned by the publisher and limited by its history and resources. This tool does not guarantee a sample count. Retained data is available to a late joiner only when both endpoints use transient local and the writer still has it.

Try a prediction first

Set a reliable, transient-local publisher. Which subscriber choices fail under these two policies? None: all four combinations are compatible, though historical delivery differs.

02 / timing contract

Is the measurement still usable?

Hold the decision time fixed at 1,000 ms. Adjust the acquisition timestamp and acceptable age. Both timestamps must share the same clock domain.

Acquisition timestamp925 ms
Decision timestamp1,000 ms

Bar shows nonnegative age on a fixed 0–500 ms scale.

Age is cumulative

The queued-sample preset is 8 ms acquisition/encoding + 12 ms transport + 20 ms queueing + 35 ms inference = 75 ms. Measuring inference alone misses 40 ms of the path.

The policy

age = decision_time − acquisition_time accepted = finite inputs AND limit ≥ 0 AND 0 ≤ age ≤ limit

Three different failures

Stale: the observation is older than the declared limit.
Future stamp: clock alignment or ordering assumptions are broken.
Invalid input: the policy cannot evaluate missing or non-finite values.

The 60 ms limit is an invented teaching value. It is not a safe timeout for any robot. This tool only classifies data; it does not stop a system or prove a reaction-time bound.

Clock choice matters

ROS simulation time can pause or jump. A host watchdog that must expire while simulation is paused needs a separately advancing elapsed-time clock. Never subtract unrelated clock domains without a justified conversion.

03 / planar geometry

Where does the tool end up?

A two-link arm in the x–y plane. Positive joint angles rotate counterclockwise. The second angle is relative to the first link, not the world.

Bent configuration

Forward kinematics

x = L₁ cos(q₁) + L₂ cos(q₁ + q₂) y = L₁ sin(q₁) + L₂ sin(q₁ + q₂) q in radians; L and output in meters |L₁ − L₂| ≤ radius ≤ L₁ + L₂

Read the picture

The dashed circles bound the ideal reachable annulus. The picture rescales when lengths change; the numeric values carry the physical scale. The gold point is the tool position.

The model omits joint limits, collision, payload, dynamics, and controller behavior. A geometrically reachable point is not necessarily an executable or safe task pose.

Why straight and folded are special

For this planar position task, the Jacobian determinant is L₁L₂ sin(q₂). At a straight or fully folded elbow it is zero: the arm loses one independent instantaneous direction of tool motion. A singularity is a geometric condition, not a claim that the robot must fall or collide.
